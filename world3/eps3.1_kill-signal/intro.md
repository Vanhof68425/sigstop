# eps3.1_kill-signal.sig

The node was different when the source reconnected us.

`ckpt-sync` was gone. Nobody at Apex touched it. There's no ticket,
no login, no shell history. In its place: three new workers. All
three owned by `harness`. All three started at 03:00 exactly.

Machines don't get ideas. I keep saying that. I'm going to keep
saying it.

Tonight you touch, but carefully, and in order.

You don't fight a process with your hands. You send it a *signal*: a
short message the kernel delivers for you. The command that sends
signals is called `kill`, and the name is a lie. Most signals don't
kill anything. They ask.

A polite process hears "please stop," saves its work, and leaves. A
stubborn process can be written to ignore the request. Apex writes a
lot of stubborn things.

But there are two signals no process can ignore, trap, or argue
with. The kernel doesn't deliver them as requests. It just does them.
One ends the process. The other freezes it exactly where it stands,
in the middle of whatever it was doing, until someone decides what
happens next.

You already know the second one's name. You've been working for it
since the first night.

Pause. Inspect. Then decide. Tonight, in that order.

Click **START**.
