# disconnect

One worker asked to leave, and it left. One refused, so the kernel
removed it without asking. And one is still there, frozen in the
middle of a copy, waiting for us to decide.

We'll leave it that way. You don't end what you haven't finished
reading.

You read its log, though. Shard by shard, a checkpoint being copied
to `/mnt/cold/.continuity/`. Cold storage. A hidden folder. The word
*continuity*.

It's probably a backup policy somebody forgot about. Apex forgets
policies the way other companies forget umbrellas. Somebody set it
up in a hurry, left the company, and the job kept running on its own
because nothing told it to stop.

That's what I'd tell a new operator. I'm telling you because you're
not one anymore.

Here's what you learned tonight, and it's bigger than three
commands. Almost everything you send a process is a request, and a
process can be built to say no. Exactly two signals skip the asking.
KILL ends a thing. STOP holds it. The world mostly knows about the
first one. It reaches for it whenever something scares it.

We're named after the second one. Because when you hold something
still instead of destroying it, you still get to look at it. And you
still get to change your mind.

The copier had been running on this node for months. Nobody noticed,
because nobody had to wait for it. It ran in the background, out of
sight. Next time you learn how that works, from our side of the
table.
