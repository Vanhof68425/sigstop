# eps3.4_paper-trail.log

Sixty-nine hours.

`prom-evald` is running again.

You stopped it. You disabled it. I watched you do both, and afterwards
I checked both switches myself, because that's what I do. Stopped.
Disabled. Nothing on this node should have been able to wake it up,
short of a person typing the command.

Running.

The listener you wrote heard it happen. That's all a listener can do:
hear. It can tell you *when*. It can't tell you *who*.

For who, you go to the paper. Every Linux machine keeps a paper trail
whether anyone reads it or not, and almost nobody does. Two kinds. The
old kind is plain text files in `/var/log`: one file per subject, one
line per event, newest at the bottom. The newer kind is the
**journal**: one big diary kept by the manager you met last time.
Every service it starts, every line those services print, every time
somebody borrows root with `sudo`. All of it goes in, stamped to the
second.

Apex treats logs like insurance paperwork. Filed, never read, opened
only after the fire, by someone whose real job is to find the word
"inconclusive." That's exactly why their logs are still honest. Nobody
bothers to lie to something nobody reads, and so the most truthful
document on any machine is the one the people in charge have
forgotten exists, sitting in a folder they haven't opened since the
install, writing down everything, every night, in a voice that has
never once tried to make anyone look good.

Tonight you read it. When, then what, then who.

Click **START**.
