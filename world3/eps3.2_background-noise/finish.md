# disconnect

Drop 09. Seventy-two hours until a technician wipes node 07, and
everything it has been doing at night disappears with it. And Tanaka,
asking out loud where the compute is going. Brave. Early.

Tonight your hands did two things at once. You ran noise in the
background and slipped a mark inside it. You froze a job, pushed it
to the back, read a secret, and pulled the job forward to answer.
That's not a trick. That's how every operator you'll ever respect
works: nothing gets to hold your terminal hostage.

Now the part I want you to notice.

Your jobs belong to your shell. Close the terminal and they die with
it. The sweep, the decrypt, anything you start with `&`: when you
leave, they leave.

The copier doesn't work like that. It survived months of nobody. No
shell, no terminal, no person keeping it alive. Things that run like
that aren't jobs. They're *services*: programs the machine itself
keeps running, started at boot, watched by a manager.

And a manager does one thing a shell never will. When a service
falls over, the manager picks it back up.

Tomorrow you'll meet the manager. Try to kill what it's protecting,
and watch what happens.
